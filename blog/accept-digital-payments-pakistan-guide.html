How to Accept Digital Payments in Pakistan: The Complete 2026 Guide for Merchants

Every acceptance method — QR/Raast, cards, wallets, POS, SoftPOS and payment links — what each costs, and how to choose the right mix for your business.

Short answer: a Pakistani business can accept digital payments five ways — Raast QR codes (instant, interoperable, near-free with the government subsidy), card acceptance via POS terminals or tap-on-phone SoftPOS apps, mobile wallets, an online payment gateway for a website or app, and remote payment links sent over WhatsApp or SMS. Most merchants now start with QR plus SoftPOS, then add a terminal as card volume grows.

Why 2026 is the year to go digital

Pakistan’s shift to digital payments has moved from encouragement to mandate. Three things changed:

  • The State Bank of Pakistan is mandating QR acceptance for retail outlets in 2026 as part of its cashless-economy programme. QR is becoming a compliance requirement, not an option.
  • A Rs 3.5 billion government subsidy (approved September 2025) pays merchants to accept QR. Under the scheme, merchants can be reimbursed up to 0.5% of each qualifying QR transaction — effectively free acceptance for small merchants.
  • Adoption is compounding. According to SBP data, the number of merchants accepting QR payments rose roughly 245% in the last fiscal year, and JazzCash alone reported about 1.9 million interoperable Raast QR merchants by October 2026 (Business Recorder).

For a shop owner, this means customers will increasingly expect to pay by scanning a code or tapping a card — and the government is subsidising you to say yes.

QR payments and Raast

What Raast is: Raast is Pakistan’s instant payment system, built and operated by the State Bank of Pakistan. It moves money between bank accounts in seconds, and its person-to-merchant (P2M) QR standard is interoperable — a single QR sticker at your counter works with every Pakistani bank and wallet app, so you don’t need a separate code per wallet.

Why merchants like it: payments settle instantly, there’s no hardware to buy, and costs are low to zero — reinforced by the 0.5% government reimbursement while the subsidy scheme lasts. To reduce “did it go through?” anxiety at the counter, devices like the Tapsys Soundbox announce each payment aloud the moment it lands.

How to start: apply through a payment provider or your bank. You’ll complete merchant KYC (CNIC and business details), receive your QR stand or sticker, and funds settle to your business account. Setup is typically measured in days, not weeks.

Card acceptance: POS, mPOS and SoftPOS

Cards still matter — especially for larger tickets and customers who don’t maintain wallet balances. You have three hardware paths:

  • POS terminal — the classic countertop machine. Look for compact models that work on WiFi and mobile data with all-day battery (like Tapsys POS) so you’re not tied to a phone line or counter position.
  • Mobile POS (mPOS) — a small card reader paired with your phone. Ideal for delivery fleets and businesses that sell in more than one place.
  • SoftPOS / tap-on-phone — an app (such as the Tapsys Merchant Mobile App) that turns an NFC-enabled Android phone into a contactless card terminal. No hardware purchase, no rental; you can start accepting tap cards, wallets, QR and Raast from one app.

Domestic and international card acceptance are priced differently — confirm both rates, plus settlement timing, with your provider before signing.

Online payment gateways

If you sell through a website or app, a payment gateway lets customers check out with cards and wallets. When comparing gateways, look at: supported methods (domestic vs international cards, wallets, Raast), settlement cycle, refund handling, integration effort (plugins vs API), and whether the provider is PCI DSS certified. Tapsys provides gateway acceptance alongside in-person methods, so online and offline sales settle into one merchant account.

Payment links for WhatsApp and COD

You don’t need a website to collect remotely. Payment links (payment requests) let you send a payment URL to a customer by SMS, email, Messenger or WhatsApp; they tap, pay by card or wallet, and you get confirmation. Use cases that work well in Pakistan:

  • WhatsApp/Instagram sellers taking orders in DMs
  • COD sellers collecting on delivery without carrying cash
  • Service businesses invoicing clients (tuition, tailors, clinics, agencies)

The SBP 2026 QR mandate and the subsidy

Under the cashless-economy push, SBP is moving to make QR acceptance standard for retail outlets in 2026. In parallel, the Rs 3.5 billion subsidy approved in September 2025 reimburses merchants for transaction costs — up to 0.5% of each qualifying QR transaction. Practical implications:

  1. If you haven’t deployed a QR code yet, treat 2026 as your deadline, not a nice-to-have.
  2. Ask your provider to confirm you’re set up to benefit from the reimbursement scheme.
  3. An interoperable Raast QR (not a single-wallet code) is the future-proof choice — one sticker, every app.

Getting-started checklist

  1. Choose a provider that offers QR, cards and links under one merchant relationship (fewer settlements to reconcile).
  2. Prepare KYC documents: CNIC, business proof and bank account details.
  3. Confirm pricing in writing: setup, per-method MDR, and settlement cycle.
  4. Deploy QR visibly at the counter; consider a Soundbox for instant audio confirmation.
  5. Add card acceptance (SoftPOS first — zero hardware — then a terminal if card volume justifies it).
  6. Train staff: how to guide a customer to scan, what a confirmed payment looks like, and refund handling.
  7. Verify you’re enrolled for the QR cost-reimbursement scheme.

Frequently asked questions

How do I accept QR payments at my shop in Pakistan?
Sign up with a payment provider or your bank for a Raast person-to-merchant QR code. You receive a QR stand or sticker for your counter; customers scan it with any bank or wallet app and money settles to your account instantly. Providers like Tapsys also offer a Soundbox device that announces each payment aloud.
What is Raast and why does it matter for merchants?
Raast is the State Bank of Pakistan’s instant payment system. Raast QR is interoperable — one QR code works with every Pakistani bank and wallet app — and person-to-merchant payments are instant and low-cost, with a government subsidy reimbursing merchants up to 0.5% per transaction while the scheme lasts.
Can I accept card payments without a POS machine?
Yes. SoftPOS (tap-on-phone) apps like the Tapsys Merchant Mobile App turn an NFC-enabled Android phone into a card acceptance terminal, so you can accept contactless cards and wallets with no hardware purchase or rental.
How can I collect payments on WhatsApp without a website?
Use payment links or payment requests: you send a link by SMS, email, Messenger or WhatsApp, the customer taps and pays by card or wallet, and you get confirmation. Tapsys merchants can send payment requests directly from their merchant account.
Is accepting digital payments expensive for small merchants?
QR acceptance is close to free: Raast QR is low or zero cost, and the government’s Rs 3.5 billion subsidy (approved September 2025) reimburses up to 0.5% per qualifying QR transaction. Card acceptance carries higher fees, so many small merchants start with QR and add cards as demand grows.
