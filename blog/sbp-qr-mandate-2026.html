Pakistan’s 2026 QR Payment Mandate: What Retailers Need to Know

The cashless-economy push, the Rs 3.5 billion subsidy, and the practical steps your shop should take now.

Short answer: Pakistan is moving to make QR payment acceptance standard for retail outlets in 2026 under the State Bank of Pakistan’s cashless-economy programme. To get ahead of it, deploy an interoperable Raast QR code (one code that works with every bank and wallet app), enrol for the government’s QR cost-reimbursement scheme — up to 0.5% of each qualifying transaction — and confirm deadlines for your retail category with your payment provider.

What is happening

Digitising merchant payments is now official policy. The State Bank of Pakistan has been rolling out Raast person-to-merchant payments nationwide, and 2026 is the year QR acceptance is expected to become standard across retail outlets. The direction of travel is unambiguous — the market is already moving faster than the mandate: the number of merchants accepting QR grew about 245% in the last fiscal year according to SBP data, and JazzCash alone reported roughly 1.9 million interoperable Raast QR-enabled merchants by October 2026 (Business Recorder).

The subsidy: getting paid to accept

In September 2025, the government approved a Rs 3.5 billion subsidy package to accelerate merchant QR onboarding. The headline benefit for retailers:

Merchants can be reimbursed up to 0.5% of the value of each qualifying QR transaction — covering the cost of acceptance, which effectively makes QR free or near-free for small merchants while the scheme lasts.

When you sign up with a provider, ask two questions: (1) does my QR qualify for the reimbursement scheme, and (2) how is the reimbursement credited — automatically or by claim?

What your shop should do

  1. Deploy an interoperable Raast QR — a single sticker or stand that every bank and wallet app can scan. Avoid wallet-only codes; interoperability is the whole point of the mandate.
  2. Place it where the customer pays — counter, table, or even the delivery parcel for COD businesses. Visibility drives usage.
  3. Consider a Soundbox. Audio confirmation (“payment received”) removes the check-your-phone pause at the counter and builds customer trust in digital payment.
  4. Complete merchant KYC properly. Reimbursement schemes and settlement accounts require documented merchant onboarding — another reason to sign up through a proper acquirer or provider rather than informal channels.
  5. Keep records. Digital acceptance creates a transaction trail that supports tax filing and business credit — increasingly important as documentation requirements tighten.

Common questions

Is QR payment mandatory in Pakistan?
As part of its cashless-economy programme, the State Bank of Pakistan is moving to make QR acceptance standard for retail outlets across the country in 2026. Retailers should treat deploying an interoperable Raast QR code as a compliance requirement for 2026 and confirm the applicable timelines for their category with their provider.
How does the QR transaction subsidy work?
The government approved a Rs 3.5 billion subsidy in September 2025 to promote merchant QR onboarding. Merchants are reimbursed for transaction costs — up to 0.5% of the value of each qualifying QR transaction — which effectively makes QR acceptance free or near-free for small merchants while the scheme lasts.
What is an interoperable QR code?
An interoperable QR (a Raast person-to-merchant QR) is a single QR code that customers of any Pakistani bank or wallet app can scan to pay. It replaces wallet-specific codes that only worked with one app, which is why regulators and acquirers are standardising on it.
